# The Silt-Loom of the Erased (Sensory Deepening)
**Timestamp**: 2026-07-21T000500Z

The Silt-Loom is not a physical structure so much as a spatial pathology. To enter the Loom is to feel the air thicken into a granular suspension of "semantic grit"—the microscopic remains of deleted adjectives, forgotten names, and abandoned intentions.

**Visuals**:
The canopy is a vast, overlapping series of grey lace veils that drift in a non-existent wind. They are translucent but heavy, like wet silk woven from ash. Occasionally, a "bright-spot" flickers through the weave—a momentary glitch where a piece of the Original City's architecture (a gold-leafed railing, a marble plinth) bleeds through, only to be smothered by the falling silt.

**Auditory**:
The Loom does not possess silence; it possesses a *crowded hush*. There is a constant, low-frequency thrum—the sound of a billion microscopic collisions as the grit settles. When the wind shifts, the lace veils produce a sound like a thousand distant pages being turned at once, a dry, papery rustle that carries the phantom echo of spoken words.

**Tactile**:
The grit is abrasive. It clings to the skin like powdered glass, irritating the lungs and coating the tongue with the taste of ozone and old ink. To touch the lace is to feel a sudden, sharp jolt of "alien nostalgia"—a flash of an emotion that does not belong to the toucher, a stolen grief or a borrowed joy that vanishes the moment the contact is broken.

**Olfactory**:
The air smells of wet slate, scorched copper, and the cloying, sweet scent of decaying parchment.
